About Aerotherm

A materials company. A focused purpose.

Founded in October 2026 in Pyeongtaek, Republic of Korea, Aerotherm researches and develops nano-silica boards made with aerogel.

Our flagship product, also called Aerotherm, goes between the cells of the rechargeable batteries used in EVs and ESS. When a battery fault sends one cell into thermal runaway, the board is there to suppress or stop thermal propagation to the neighboring cells and to neighboring ESS containers.

Where Aerotherm differs: thermal radiation

Typical aerogel insulation mainly suppresses heat transfer by convection. Aerotherm keeps that and is also highly effective at suppressing heat transfer by thermal radiation.

That matters because radiative heat transfer rises with the fourth power of absolute temperature. At thermal-runaway temperatures, radiation becomes a major path for heat between cells.

Technology

Engineered across heat paths.

A nanoporous silica network limits convection and conduction. Infrared opacifiers mixed into the nano-silica take on radiation, the heat path that rises with the fourth power of absolute temperature.

Nanopores & the Knudsen effect

The pores inside the board are smaller than the mean free path of air molecules, about 70 nm at room temperature. Molecules hit the pore walls far more often than each other, so convection and conduction through the air are strongly suppressed.

Low solid conduction

Silica (SiO2) conducts heat poorly, and the board's porous solid structure leaves heat very few paths through the solid.

Radiation blocking

Where Aerotherm differs

Typical aerogel insulation mainly suppresses heat transfer by convection. Aerotherm keeps that, and infrared opacifiers mixed into the nano-silica reflect or scatter the radiant heat produced at high temperatures.

Radiative heat transfer rises with the fourth power of absolute temperature, so at thermal-runaway temperatures this is a major path for heat between cells.
